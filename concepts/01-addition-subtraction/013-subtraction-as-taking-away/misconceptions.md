# Common Misconceptions: Subtraction as Taking Away

### 1. Counting the Removed Items Instead of the Remaining Set
* **The Trap**: When asked *"5 take away 2"*, the child points to the 2 items that were removed and answers *"2"*.
* **Why it happens**: The child focuses on the action of removing items rather than the remaining collection.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"You took away 2! Excellent. Now look at the basket—how many items are still inside?"*

---

### 2. Order Confusion (Thinking $A - B$ is the same as $B - A$)
* **The Trap**: The child tries to subtract the larger number from the smaller number or assumes order doesn't matter (like in addition).
* **Why it happens**: Commutativity from addition ($3 + 5 = 5 + 3$) is incorrectly applied to subtraction.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"If we have 3 apples, can we eat 5 apples? No! We always start with the big pile first."*

---

### 3. Recounting the Whole Group First
* **The Trap**: After removing items, the child recounts from 1 starting with the missing items.
* **Why it happens**: Lack of confidence in identifying the remaining subset directly.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"Cover up the items we took away with your hand. Just count what is visible under your eyes."*
