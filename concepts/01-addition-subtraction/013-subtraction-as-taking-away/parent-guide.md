# Parent Guide: Subtraction as Taking Away

## 2-Minute Overview
Subtraction at this stage is all about **taking away** items from a starting group. Children learn that when we take items away from a collection, the remaining count becomes smaller than the starting total. Using visual objects (like apples, toys, or counters on a ten-frame) helps children see the "part-whole" relationship: starting with a Whole, removing a Part, and leaving the remaining Part.

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: Snack Take-Away 🍎
1. Place 6 small snacks (e.g., raisins, berries, or crackers) on a plate.
2. Ask your child to count the total starting amount (*"We have 6 raisins!"*).
3. Have your child "take away" 2 raisins and eat or set them aside.
4. Ask: *"How many raisins are left on the plate now?"*
5. Help them phrase it aloud: *"6 take away 2 leaves 4!"*

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: Toy Rescue Mission 🧸
- Put 8 small toys or blocks inside a box or circle.
- Roll a die (or draw a number card 1–5).
- Have your child "rescue" (remove) that many toys from the box.
- Ask them to write or say the subtraction equation (e.g. $8 - 3 = 5$).

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **What to look for**: Does your child count all remaining items one by one, or can they quickly subitise/recognise the leftover amount?
- **Encouragement Tip**: If your child hesitates, remind them to count what is *left*, not what was taken away!

---

## What NOT to Do
- 🚫 **Do not force quick memorization**: Allow them to physically take items away or cross them off visually.
- 🚫 **Do not use timers or speed pressure**: Keep the atmosphere calm, encouraging, and playful.
- 🚫 **Do not confuse subtraction with addition**: Emphasize that taking away makes the collection smaller.
