# Parent Guide: Subtraction on Number Line

## 2-Minute Overview
Using a number line for subtraction transforms abstract equations into a physical journey. Instead of removing objects from a pile, children learn that subtraction means **walking backward** along a numbered path toward 0. Starting at the total number and hopping to the left helps children visualize distance and difference clearly.

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: Backward Frog Jumps 🐸
1. Draw a chalk line on the floor or pavement with numbers 0 to 10 spaced out evenly.
2. Ask your child to stand on number **8**.
3. Tell them: *"Hop backward 3 steps like a frog! What number did you land on?"*
4. Repeat with different starting numbers ($7 - 2 = 5$, $9 - 4 = 5$).

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: Ruler Backward Hop 📏
- Take a standard 15cm ruler or tape measure.
- Place a toy car on mark **9**.
- Slide the car backward by 4 centimeters while counting out loud: *"8, 7, 6, 5!"*
- Ask your child to state the final equation: *"9 minus 4 equals 5!"*

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **What to look for**: Does your child count the starting notch line as step 1? Remind them to count the *hops* (the spaces moved), not the starting mark line.

---

## What NOT to Do
- 🚫 **Do not jump forward**: Moving right increases the number (addition). Emphasize that subtraction always moves **left/backward** toward zero.
- 🚫 **Do not rush the hops**: Encourage slow, rhythmic hops to build motor-cognitive links.
