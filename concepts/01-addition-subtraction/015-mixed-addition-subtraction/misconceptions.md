# Common Misconceptions: Mixed Addition & Subtraction

### 1. Sign Blindness (Ignoring $+$ vs $-$)
* **The Trap**: The child adds every problem by default, turning $7 - 3 = 4$ into $7 + 3 = 10$.
* **Why it happens**: Addition was learned first, creating a habit to combine numbers regardless of the operator.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"Look closely at the sign! Is it a Plus (+) to join together, or a Minus (-) to take away?"*

---

### 2. Not Connecting Fact Families
* **The Trap**: The child solves $4 + 5 = 9$ easily, but treats $9 - 5 = ?$ as a completely new and unrelated problem.
* **Why it happens**: Viewing addition and subtraction as isolated facts rather than inverse sides of the same part-whole relationship.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"Remember the 9-5-4 triangle! If 4 and 5 make 9, then taking 5 away leaves..."*

---

### 3. Subtracting in Reverse Order
* **The Trap**: Seeing $2 - 6 = ?$ and attempting to calculate $6 - 2 = 4$.
* **Why it happens**: Applying addition commutativity ($2 + 6 = 6 + 2$) to subtraction.
* **Parent Verbal Prompt**: *"We start with the total group at the beginning! You can't take 6 apples from a plate of 2."*
