# Parent Guide: Mixed Addition & Subtraction

## 2-Minute Overview
Now that your child understands combining items ($+$) and taking items away ($-$), it's time to bring them together! This stage focuses on **fact families**—showing children that addition and subtraction are two sides of the same coin. For example, knowing that $3 + 2 = 5$ means your child also knows that $5 - 2 = 3$!

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: Fact Family Triangle 🔺
1. Draw a simple triangle on paper with the number **7** at the top point, and **4** and **3** at the bottom two corners.
2. Cover the **7** and ask: *"What do 4 and 3 make when we add them together?"* ($4 + 3 = 7$).
3. Now cover the **3** and ask: *"If we start with 7 and take away 4, what is left?"* ($7 - 4 = 3$).
4. Try with other combinations up to 10 (e.g. 6, 4, 2).

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: Plus/Minus Card Flip 🃏
- Prepare cards marked with $+$ and $-$, plus number cards 1–5.
- Draw two number cards (e.g. 5 and 3) and flip an operation card.
- If $+$ is flipped, combine them ($5 + 3 = 8$). If $-$ is flipped, take the smaller from the larger ($5 - 3 = 2$).
- Have your child say the complete equation out loud!

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **What to look for**: Does your child pause to check the operational symbol ($+$ vs $-$)? Encourage them to always check whether the sign is asking them to *add more* or *take away*.

---

## What NOT to Do
- 🚫 **Do not introduce negative numbers**: Subtraction at this stage always starts with the larger total group.
- 🚫 **Do not rush calculation speed**: Celebrate when your child notices connections like *"Hey, 4+2 is 6, so 6-2 is 4!"*
