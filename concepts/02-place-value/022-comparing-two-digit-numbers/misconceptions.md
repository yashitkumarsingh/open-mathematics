# Educator Guide: Misconceptions in Comparing Numbers

Diagnose and address common logic errors during two-digit number comparisons.

---

## 1. Ones Digit Bias (e.g., $19 > 21$)
* **The Mistake**: Faced with 19 and 21, the child selects 19 as the larger number.
* **Cognitive Break**: The child compares the individual digits in isolation, focusing on the largest single digit they see (9). They do not understand that the value of a digit is determined by its position (place value).
* **Correction Strategy**:
  - Show the values visually. Place 1 Ten rod and 9 Ones blocks on the left, and 2 Ten rods and 1 Ones block on the right.
  - Ask: *"Would you rather have 2 whole bars of chocolate (20), or 1 bar of chocolate and 9 crumbs (19)? (2 bars)."*
  - Emphasize: *"Tens are bars; Ones are crumbs. 2 bars is always more than 1 bar, even with crumbs!"*

---

## 2. Reversing inequality symbols ($<$ vs. $>$)
* **The Mistake**: The child identifies the larger number correctly but writes the wrong inequality sign (e.g. writing $25 < 12$).
* **Cognitive Break**: The child is struggling to match their verbal logic with abstract mathematical symbols.
* **Correction Strategy**:
  - Use the "Hungry Alligator/Monster" analogy. Draw eyes and teeth inside the inequality symbol: `>`.
  - Explain: *"The alligator is very hungry. It always opens its mouth wide toward the larger group of blocks because it wants to eat the most!"*
