# Parent Guide: Comparing Numbers / Guía para Padres: Comparar Números

This guide helps you teach your child how to compare two-digit numbers using place value columns.

---

## 🇺🇸 English Guide

### ⏰ The 2-Minute Concept Explanation
When children compare numbers, they sometimes get confused by high digits in the ones place. For example, they might look at 19 and 21 and think 19 is larger because 9 is much bigger than 1 or 2.
We teach them **Tens Priority**: always check the Tens column first! 2 tens (20) is always larger than 1 ten (10), no matter how many loose ones are left over. If the tens are the same (like 25 and 27), *then* we compare the ones.

### 🎲 The 5-Minute Everyday Activity
**Items needed**: Straws or pasta rods, rubber bands, and a piece of paper split into two halves (Left vs. Right).
1. Make representations of two numbers on the paper (e.g. place 2 bundles and 1 stick on the left (21), and 1 bundle and 9 sticks on the right (19)).
2. Ask: *"How many tens on the left? (2). How many tens on the right? (1). Which side has more tens? (Left). So, is 21 larger or smaller than 19? (Larger)."*
3. Connect it to the "hungry alligator" symbol: $21 > 19$. The alligator's open mouth always wants to eat the bigger pile!

---

## 🇪🇸 Guía en Español

### ⏰ Explicación Conceptual de 2 Minutos
Al comparar números de dos dígitos, siempre debemos revisar primero la columna de las **Decenas**. 2 decenas (20) siempre representan más que 1 decena (10), sin importar cuántas unidades sueltas queden. Si las decenas son iguales (como 25 y 27), *entonces* comparamos las unidades.

### 🎲 Actividad Práctica de 5 Minutos
1. Representa dos números usando pajitas (p. ej., 21 a la izquierda y 19 a la derecha).
2. Pregunta: *"¿De qué lado hay más decenas? (Izquierda). Entonces, ¿el 21 es mayor o menor que el 19? (Mayor)"*.
3. Dibuja el símbolo del cocodrilo hambriento: $21 > 19$. ¡La boca abierta del cocodrilo siempre quiere comer la cantidad más grande!
