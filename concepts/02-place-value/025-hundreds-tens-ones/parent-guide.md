# Parent Guide: Hundreds, Tens, and Ones

## 2-Minute Overview
Welcome to 3-digit numbers! Expanding from tens to **hundreds** is a major milestone for young mathematicians. Children learn that 10 bundles of Ten link together to form a big square flat of **100**. Seeing numbers like **352** broken down into **3 Hundreds, 5 Tens, and 2 Ones** ($300 + 50 + 2$) makes big numbers easy to visualize.

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: Money House 💵
1. Use toy bills or paper squares marked **$100**, **$10**, and **$1**.
2. Give your child **$352** ($3 \times \$100$, $5 \times \$10$, $2 \times \$1$).
3. Ask them to sort the bills into 3 piles: Hundreds, Tens, and Ones.
4. Have them count out loud: *"300... 350... 352!"*

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: Secret Code Builder 🕵️‍♂️
- Write 3-digit numbers on cards (e.g. 418, 635, 790).
- Ask your child to decode the secret recipe for each number:
  - *"418 = 4 Hundreds + 1 Ten + 8 Ones!"*

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **What to look for**: Does your child write $300 + 50 + 2 = 300502$? Remind them that place value slots collapse together: the 5 tens sit in the middle seat, and 2 ones sit in the end seat!

---

## What NOT to Do
- 🚫 **Do not skip zero placeholders**: Teach that numbers like 405 have **0 Tens** ($400 + 0 + 5$).
- 🚫 **Do not introduce 4-digit thousands yet**: Master hundreds up to 999 first.
