# Common Misconceptions: Skip Counting (2, 5, 10)

## 1. Off-By-One Sequence Slip
* **The Thinking Trap**: The child forgets the step size mid-sequence and shifts to counting by ones (e.g. *"5, 10, 15, 16, 17"* or *"2, 4, 6, 7, 8"*).
* **Why it Happens**: Working memory overload when keeping track of both the step increment and the current total.
* **Parent Verbal Correction Prompt**:
  > *"Listen to the rhythm! When we count by 5s, every number ends in 5 or 0. Let's start at 5 and jump by 5 again: 5, 10, 15... what comes next?"*

---

## 2. Step Size Confusion (Mixing 2s, 5s, and 10s)
* **The Thinking Trap**: Switching between step sizes accidentally within the same problem (e.g. *"2, 4, 6, 10, 15, 20"*).
* **Why it Happens**: The child remembers familiar number milestones (like 10 or 15) and jumps straight to them regardless of the rule.
* **Parent Verbal Correction Prompt**:
  > *"We are taking steps of 2 right now! Imagine taking small 2-inch frog hops. 6 plus 2 is 8, not 10!"*

---

## 3. Boundary Crossing Hangups (e.g. 18 -> 20 or 45 -> 50)
* **The Thinking Trap**: Freezing when crossing a multiple of ten (e.g. knowing 10, 12, 14, 16, 18, but hesitating at 20).
* **Why it Happens**: Decade transitions require changing both the tens and ones digits simultaneously.
* **Parent Verbal Correction Prompt**:
  > *"After 18, add 2 more. 18 plus 2 completes a new ten: 20!"*
