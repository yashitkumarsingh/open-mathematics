# Parent Guide: Skip Counting (2, 5, 10)

## 2-Minute Concept Overview
Skip counting is counting forward by a number other than 1. When children skip count by 2s ($2, 4, 6, 8...$), 5s ($5, 10, 15, 20...$), or 10s ($10, 20, 30...$), they are building the foundation for multiplication and division. Instead of counting individual items one by one, they begin grouping equal amounts into mentally manageable steps.

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: "Pair & Coin Hop"
- **Materials**: 10 pairs of socks, or a stack of 5-cent / 10-cent coins.
- **How to Play**:
  1. Lay out pairs of socks on the floor.
  2. Have your child touch each pair and count by 2s: *"2, 4, 6, 8, 10!"*
  3. Swap to coins: stack coins in piles of 5 or 10 and count the piles out loud.

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: "The Missing Rhythm"
- Clap out a skip counting rhythm together: Clap-Clap **2**, Clap-Clap **4**, Clap-Clap **6**...
- Pause mid-sequence (e.g., *"10, 15, __, 25"*) and ask your child to fill in the missing number instantly by following the pattern.

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **Watch out for**: Does your child revert to whisper-counting by ones between steps? Encourage them to feel the rhythm of the sequence.
- **Pattern recognition**: Help them notice that skip counting by 5s always ends in **5** or **0**, while skip counting by 10s always ends in **0**.

---

## What NOT to Do
- ❌ **Don't rush to formal multiplication symbols ($2 \times 4 = 8$) yet.** Focus on equal jumps and pattern awareness first.
- ❌ **Don't treat missing a step as a mistake.** Re-start the rhythm together from the beginning of the sequence.
