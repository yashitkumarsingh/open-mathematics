# Parent Guide: Arrays & Multiplication

## 2-Minute Concept Overview
An **array** is a grid of objects organized in neat horizontal rows and vertical columns. Arrays are one of the most powerful visual tools in early mathematics because they connect equal groups to geometry. Even better, arrays visually prove why $3 \times 4$ is equal to $4 \times 3$ (the commutative property)!

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: "Egg Carton & Baking Pan Grids"
- **Materials**: An egg carton (2 rows of 6), a muffin pan (3 rows of 4), or a chocolate bar grid.
- **How to Play**:
  1. Show your child a 3-by-4 muffin pan.
  2. Ask them to trace across the **rows** with their finger: *"There are 3 rows."*
  3. Ask them to trace down the **columns**: *"There are 4 columns."*
  4. Count the total cups by skip counting rows ($4, 8, 12$) or columns ($3, 6, 9, 12$).
  5. Rotate the pan $90^\circ$ and observe that the total is still 12!

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: "Sticker Array Builder"
- Give your child 12 stickers and a piece of grid paper.
- Challenge them to build as many different rectangular arrays for 12 as possible:
  - 1 row of 12 ($1 \times 12$)
  - 2 rows of 6 ($2 \times 6$)
  - 3 rows of 4 ($3 \times 4$)

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **Row vs Column memory trick**: **R**ows go **R**ight-to-left (horizontal); **C**olumns hold up **C**apitol buildings (vertical towers).
- **Corner count double check**: Ensure your child counts rows and columns from 1, without skipping the top-left corner item!

---

## What NOT to Do
- ❌ **Don't penalize order**: $3 \times 4$ and $4 \times 3$ describe the exact same quantity. Celebrate when your child notices this!
- ❌ **Don't let dots bleed together**: Keep objects neatly aligned in rows and columns so the grid pattern is unmistakable.
