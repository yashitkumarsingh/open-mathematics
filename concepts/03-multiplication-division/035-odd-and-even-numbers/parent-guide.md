# Parent Guide: Odd and Even Numbers

## 2-Minute Concept Overview
Odd and even numbers describe whether a quantity can be split into exact pairs of 2.
- **Even numbers** ($2, 4, 6, 8, 10...$) match up perfectly into buddy pairs.
- **Odd numbers** ($1, 3, 5, 7, 9...$) always leave **1 lone item** without a partner.
Building this physical buddy-pair mental model gives children a concrete understanding before they memorize digit rules.

---

## 5-Minute Everyday Activity: "Sock Pair Detective"
- **Materials**: 7 to 10 single socks.
- **How to Play**:
  1. Hand your child a pile of 7 socks.
  2. Ask them to match the socks into buddy pairs side-by-side.
  3. Notice that 3 pairs are formed, with **1 sock left over without a buddy!**
  4. Ask: *"Is 7 odd or even?"* (**Odd**, because 1 sock is left out!).
  5. Add 1 more sock (total 8) and pair them up again. All socks have buddies! (**Even**).

---

## 10-Minute Extension Challenge: "The Ones Digit Secret"
- Lay out cards with two-digit numbers (e.g. 14, 21, 28, 19).
- Teach your child the secret: *"You only need to look at the VERY LAST digit!"*
  - Ends in **0, 2, 4, 6, 8** $\rightarrow$ **EVEN**
  - Ends in **1, 3, 5, 7, 9** $\rightarrow$ **ODD**

---

## Parent Observation Guide
- **Pairing strategy**: Watch if your child moves items 2-by-2.
- **Zero check**: Explain that **0** is even because if you have 0 items, there are 0 leftovers!

---

## What NOT to Do
- ❌ **Don't rush to abstract rules before physical pairing.** Make sure they see the "odd one out" visually first!
- ❌ **Don't let them look at the tens digit.** Remind them that 27 ends in 7 (odd), even though 2 (tens place) is an even digit!
